Why I Built Stopline

Published Nov 24, 2026. A note from Jay Urena on why Stopline exists. Educational only, not financial advice.

Most of the numbers that decide whether a prop-firm account lives or dies are not on the trading screen. They live in a help center: trailing drawdown, consistency, daily loss, profit target, payout rules. The pages are long. The wording is not always consistent from one page to the next. And the rules change.

I got tired of piecing that together from forums, undated blogs, and screenshots that might have been true last quarter. I wanted one place where the math was visible, the rules were pulled from each firm's own pages, and every claim had a date on it. That is why I built Stopline.

I am Jay Urena, a disabled U.S. Army veteran and former intelligence analyst. I have traded stocks, perpetual futures, and crypto since 2018. The site is personal and independent. It is not owned by, paid by, or affiliated with any prop firm, broker, or exchange, and it has no paid placements.

The habit I kept from intelligence work

In that work, a claim without a source and a date is just a rumor. I try to build trading tools the same way.

Every prop-firm guide and calculator preset on Stopline is taken from the firm's own help center or rules page, not from forums or other review sites. Each guide shows the date the rules were last checked and links to the page used. When a firm's wording is unclear, or two of its pages disagree, the guide says so instead of guessing and points you to the firm's support.

That will never be as fast as an unofficial recap that hits the same day. It is slower on purpose. I would rather be a day late and accurate than first with a number I cannot stand behind.

The firms on the site today are Apex Trader Funding, Topstep, MyFundedFutures, Tradeify, Take Profit Trader, and Lucid Trading. I re-check the guides against the source pages regularly, and the "Updated" date at the top changes when they are. If you catch a change before I do, tell me.

What the tools do

Stopline is a set of free risk calculators that run in your browser. You do not need an account. The trade numbers you type into a calculator stay in your browser and are not sent to our servers. There are tools for position size, risk/reward, options and futures P&L, expectancy, drawdown recovery, and the three prop-firm checks the site is built around: consistency, trailing drawdown, and profit-target pace. There is also an optional trade journal, saved in your browser unless you choose to sign in and sync it.

Each calculator shows the formula and a worked example you can check by hand. Firm presets only fill in the published inputs. The math is the same for every firm.

Here is one example from the trailing drawdown calculator, using the same numbers the live page uses for an Apex 50K Intraday Performance Account. These figures are illustrative, not a recommendation, and I re-checked that preset against Apex's own pages on November 24, 2026.

A trailing drawdown follows your highest balance up. The liquidation level is highest balance - max drawdown. It rises as you make new highs and never falls back. On this Apex performance account, the max trailing drawdown is $2,000, and the floor stops rising at starting balance plus $100.

Say your starting balance is $50,000, your peak is $51,200, and you are now at $50,800.

  • Liquidation level: $51,200 - $2,000 = $49,200
  • Room left: $50,800 - $49,200 = $1,600
  • The floor stops rising at $50,100, once your peak reaches $52,100

That last point is the part people miss. A $1,200 open run-up already lifted the floor. Giving back some of the profit does not lower it. If you later make a new high at $52,100, the floor locks at $50,100 and stays there. You can open the same example and change the numbers.

What I learned from testing ideas honestly

I also research trading systems, and I try to hold that work to the same standard. Write the hypothesis down. Collect evidence. Try to break the result. Say how confident you are.

A public write-up of one paper-trading project is on GitHub: a case study on pricing short-horizon crypto prediction markets. It publishes no strategy parameters, and no real money was risked on it.

The most useful result so far is about method, not profit. An early version looked promising. The cause turned out to be a stale data feed. The old prices made the model's disagreement with the market look larger than it was. After I fixed the feed, I restarted the evidence clock rather than carry those numbers forward. That was uncomfortable, and it was the right call.

A few other findings from that write-up, stated plainly: a chart-pattern bot lost money after fees and was retired. A historical test on a second venue's data also lost money. Several candidate edges disappeared once realistic fees and partial fills were applied. Fees matter more than intuition suggests.

That matches a broader lesson I have taken from testing simple rules: a lot of them look fine until you put realistic costs on them. I would rather publish the miss than dress up a backtest.

The Learn section is written in that spirit. There are new guides on how to read candlesticks, basic candlestick patterns, and common indicators. The patterns guide does not stop at the textbook names. It includes an evidence section. The best academic tests found little or no predictive value in US and Japanese stocks, and some practitioner statistics show patterns acting opposite their usual labels. The point is not that charts are useless. It is that a pattern is context, not a signal on its own.

If you want to use it

Stopline is at stoplineinvestor.org. Open a calculator and use it. The Learn page has the guides, including the firm rule write-ups and a side-by-side comparison. Trade Tuesday is a weekly note, Tuesday to Tuesday, on what moved and what it means for risk and size.

If a calculation looks wrong, a rule has changed, or a page is unclear, email stoplineinvestor@outlook.com or use the contact page. I read what comes in.

This is educational, not financial, investment, or legal advice, and not a recommendation to trade with any firm. Trading stocks, futures, options, and crypto involves substantial risk of loss. Always confirm a rule on the firm's own page before you rely on it.
