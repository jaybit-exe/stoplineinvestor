FTMO Futures Cuts Growth 100K and 150K Drawdown for New Evaluations

Rule changePublished Nov 19, 2026Checked Nov 19, 2026

Published Nov 19, 2026. Checked against FTMO's own trading update on Nov 19, 2026. FTMO says the new values also appear on its Futures Trading Objectives and Rules page, so check FTMO's update and that page before you buy. Stopline is not affiliated with FTMO. FTMO is not one of our firm presets yet, so the calculator links below use Custom values.

What changed

In its Futures Trading Update of Nov 5, 2026, FTMO said that from Thursday, Nov 5 at 4:30 a.m. ET the Maximum Drawdown and Payout Eligibility requirements of its Growth 100K and Growth 150K accounts change. They apply to all new Evaluations created on or after that time.

Growth accountRuleBeforeAfter
100KMaximum Drawdown (EOD trailing)$3,500$3,000
Payout eligibility: 4 qualifying days with a minimum daily P&L of$250$300
150KMaximum Drawdown (EOD trailing)$5,000$4,500
Payout eligibility: 4 qualifying days with a minimum daily P&L of$300$450
  • Evaluations created before Nov 5 at 4:30 a.m. ET keep the old values, including the Sim-Funded and Live accounts they lead to.
  • The Maximum Drawdown applies to Evaluation, Sim-Funded and Live Funded accounts. Payout eligibility applies to Sim-Funded accounts.
  • The Daily Loss Limit and all other Growth objectives are unchanged.
  • FTMO's stated reason: Growth is a new product being fine-tuned with real market data, and the changes keep its payout model stable.

What it means for your numbers

By our arithmetic, the old drawdowns were 3.5% (100K) and 3.33% (150K) of account size. Both are now exactly 3.0%. The room you have to be wrong shrank by $500 on the 100K and $500 on the 150K, and the payout bar for each qualifying day rose by $50 and $150.

Smaller room means smaller risk per trade. If you risk a fixed 10% of your drawdown on each trade, that is $300 on the new 100K instead of $350. With a 25-point stop on Micro E-mini Nasdaq (MNQ, $2 per point, so $50 per contract), $300 buys 6 contracts instead of 7. Your own risk percentage will differ, so run your numbers.
