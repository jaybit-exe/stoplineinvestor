Take Profit Trader's New Plans: 25K Drawdown Cut to $1,000, and a Daily Loss Limit on the EOD Plan

Rule changePublished Nov 24, 2026Checked Nov 24, 2026

Published Nov 24, 2026. Checked against Take Profit Trader's help center on Nov 24, 2026 (articles updated Nov 23). Firms change rules, so confirm on Take Profit Trader's plan comparison before you buy. Stopline is not affiliated with Take Profit Trader. Note on our presets: Stopline's Take Profit Trader presets still show the legacy rules (25K drawdown $1,500, 75K and 100K sizes). They are correct for accounts bought before Nov 23. For new accounts, use the custom links in this post.

What changed

From Nov 23, 2026, Take Profit Trader sells three plans. They share profit targets, max drawdown and contract limits by account size and differ in when the drawdown trails, whether there is a daily loss limit, and the daily profit cap in PRO:

Legacy accounts keep their rules and reset pricing while they stay active. A legacy Test can be traded and reset until its subscription expires; passing it leads to a legacy PRO account. After that, any new purchase is on one of the three new plans.

The EOD plan's daily loss limit can end the account. Per Take Profit Trader's Rule 3, the limit is tracked in real time, including open P&L and commissions. Hitting it closes your positions for the day. If your balance is at or above the starting balance, that is a soft breach and you trade again the next day. If it is below the starting balance, that is a hard breach and you need a reset.

What it means for your numbers

The new 25K is a third tighter. $1,000 of drawdown is 20 points on one ES contract ($50 a point), 200 points on MES, or 500 points on MNQ ($2 a point). For scale, ES's 14-day average true range was 211 points on Nov 23 (see this week's Trade Tuesday). One ES contract on a new 25K has room for less than a tenth of an average day's range. The target is still $1,500, so the target is now 1.5 times the drawdown, against 1 to 1 before.

On the EOD plan, the daily limit is the tighter rule early on. On a 25K EOD account, the $500 daily loss limit is half the $1,000 drawdown. Until your balance is above the starting balance, a bad day ends the account at -$500, not -$1,000. Size from the daily limit, not the drawdown: risking $100 a trade gives you five full losers before the day is over.

Intraday versus EOD trailing, in Take Profit Trader's own example: on a 50K with a $2,000 drawdown, a trade that runs to +$1,500 and closes flat leaves $500 of room with intraday trailing, and the full $2,000 with end-of-day trailing.
