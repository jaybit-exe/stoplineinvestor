Margin and leverage

Leverage, borrowed amount and the price where a margin call can hit a long stock position.

Results

How it works

Leverage is position value divided by your own cash. Borrowing magnifies gains and losses, so a 2x position loses twice as much as an unleveraged one on the same move.

A margin call can happen when your equity falls below the maintenance requirement: price = loan / (shares x (1 - maintenance %)). The 25% default is a common minimum, but brokers often require more. This covers long stock positions only, so check your broker for exact rules.

FAQ

How is the margin call price calculated? For a long stock position: loan / (shares x (1 - maintenance margin)). Below that price your equity falls under the maintenance requirement.

What maintenance margin should I enter? 25% is a common regulatory minimum for long stock, but many brokers require more, especially on volatile stocks. Use your broker's number.

Does this work for short positions or futures? No. It covers long stock positions bought on margin only.
