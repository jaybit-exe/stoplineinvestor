Trading Risk Glossary

Short definitions of the risk terms used across the calculators. Each links to a tool where it applies.

Position size
The number of shares or contracts in a trade. See the position size calculator.
Stop-loss
A planned exit price that limits your loss if the trade goes against you.
Risk per trade
The amount, often a percent of your account, you are willing to lose if the stop is hit.
R multiple
A trade result measured in units of the amount you risked. A $300 gain on $100 risked is +3R.
Reward-to-risk
Potential gain divided by potential loss on a trade. See risk/reward.
Win rate
The share of your trades that make money.
Break-even win rate
The win rate you need to make nothing, given your reward-to-risk ratio and before costs.
Expectancy
The average amount you make or lose per trade over many trades. See expectancy.
Profit factor
Total profits divided by total losses. Above 1 means winners outweigh losers.
Kelly criterion
A formula for the bet size that maximizes long-run growth. Traders often use half of it or less.
Drawdown
The fall from a peak balance to a later low, usually shown as a percent.
Trailing drawdown
A loss limit that rises with your highest balance, common on prop firm accounts.
Consistency rule
A prop firm rule limiting how much of your profit can come from a single day.
Risk of ruin
The chance of losing enough to end a plan or breach a limit. See the simulator.
Slippage
The difference between the price you expected and the price you got.
Leverage
Controlling a position larger than your own cash by borrowing. See margin and leverage.
Margin call
A broker demand to add funds or close positions when your equity falls below the maintenance requirement.
Premium
The price paid for an option, quoted per share and multiplied by 100 per contract.
Strike price
The price at which an option holder can buy or sell the underlying asset.
Break-even price
The underlying price at which an option position makes nothing at expiry. See options P&L.
