Expectancy and Kelly

What an average trade earns, and a ceiling on how much to risk.

Results

How it works

Expectancy is what you make on average per trade: win rate x average win - loss rate x average loss. A positive number means an edge across many trades, if your stats are real and the sample is large.

The Kelly formula estimates the growth-maximizing risk. Full Kelly is very aggressive, so many traders use half or less. Zero means no edge.

FAQ

What is trading expectancy? The average amount a trade makes over many trades: win rate times average win, minus loss rate times average loss. A positive number means the method has made money on average in the sample you entered.

What is the Kelly criterion? A formula for the fraction of capital that maximises long-run growth given your win rate and payoff ratio: win rate minus loss rate divided by the win/loss ratio.

Should I bet full Kelly? Most traders treat it as a ceiling, not a target. Your real win rate and payoff are estimates, and full Kelly swings hard when they are off, which is why half Kelly or less is common.
