How it works

Many prop firms require that no single winning day makes up more than a set share of your total profit, often somewhere around 30% to 50%. The check is biggest winning day / total profit.

If you are over the limit, you can usually fix it by earning more profit on smaller days without setting a new best day. The profit you need in total is biggest day / limit. Rules differ by firm and account type, and some measure only at payout time, so always confirm yours.

Below or at or below: why it matters

Firms word the line differently. Topstep, MyFundedFutures, Tradeify and Lucid count a best day exactly at the limit as passing. Apex and Take Profit Trader say your best day must be below the limit, so landing exactly on it is not enough. The preset sets this for you, and the result then says "more than" the total when the line itself does not count.

FAQ

How do I calculate a prop firm consistency rule? Divide your biggest winning day by your total profit and multiply by 100. If the result is above your firm's limit, the total profit you need is your biggest day divided by the limit, for example $1,500 / 0.50 = $3,000.

Does breaking the consistency rule fail my account? At the firms preset here, no. Their help centers say you keep trading: Topstep raises your profit target, Apex and Tradeify hold your payout until the share drops, and MyFundedFutures, Take Profit Trader and Lucid ask for more trading days or more profit.

Do losing days change my consistency percentage? Yes, when the firm uses net profit. A losing day lowers your total while your best day stays the same, so your percentage goes up. Tradeify and Apex both describe this, and Topstep notes that losses do not reset your best day.

Can I fix a consistency problem by trading more the same day? Usually not. Any extra profit made on the day you set your best day adds to that best day. Topstep says the remaining profit has to come on a later trading day.
